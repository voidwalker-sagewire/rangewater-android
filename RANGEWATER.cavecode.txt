🎮 BLOCK 3 — CURRENT ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- UI: Jetpack Compose with Kotlin 2.0.21 and Compose Compiler plugin 2.0.21
- Android: compileSdk 36, targetSdk 36, minSdk 24
- Build: AGP 8.10.1, Gradle 8.11.1, Java 17, KSP 2.0.21-1.0.28
- Map Engine: MapLibre Native Android SDK 11.5.1
- Spatial Math: MapLibre Turf Java 6.0.1
- Persistence: Android Room 2.8.5 with local SQLite and Flow-backed reads
- CI: GitHub Actions runs JVM geometry tests, Android Room instrumentation tests,
  and builds a debug APK with the committed Gradle wrapper
- Federal imagery stack:
  - Overview: cached USGS Imagery Only raster tiles through zoom 15
  - Detail: USDA FPAC NAIP dynamic exportImage requests from zoom 15 through 20
  - Navigation: cached USGS Imagery Topo labeled mode capped at zoom 16
- Water coverage engine: aggregated GeoJSON points plus Turf-generated 0-800-foot
  preferred-access disks and true 800-1,000-foot planning-transition annuli rendered
  with data-driven MapLibre Circle, Fill, and Line layers above every basemap mode
- Display preferences: platform SharedPreferences persist Full, Lines Only, or Off water
  coverage plus independent pasture-fill state without changing Room schema version 2

🪨 BLOCK 4 — SETTLED DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RangeWater is a native Android application built with Kotlin and Compose.
2. The intended package identity is com.sagewire.rangewater.
3. The application is offline-first and will use MapLibre rather than Google Maps.
4. Turf is a direct compile dependency for geodesic circle calculations.
5. The baseline grazing visualization uses a geodesic radius of 243.84 meters
   (800 feet) around a water asset.
6. Terrain barriers and slope will eventually constrain the simple radius.
7. Michael approved the federal imagery path for RangeWater.
   - Production imagery family: USDA NAIP / USGS orthoimagery.
   - Initial online source: USGS Imagery Only cached tile service.
   - Attribution: USDA, USGS – The National Map: Orthoimagery.
   - The selected CONUS imagery data are public domain and require no API key.
   - Offline redistribution will use an approved regional packaging process rather
     than unrestricted bulk caching against the live USGS service.
8. Michael approved a hybrid federal imagery presentation after device testing.
   - USGS cached imagery remains the regional overview source.
   - USDA FPAC NAIP is the close-detail source beginning at zoom 15.
   - Direct USDA browser testing showed sufficient detail to distinguish lane
     markings, building shadows, and roof seams at the inspected location.
   - USGS Imagery Topo supplies an optional labeled navigation mode.
   - Aerial/Labeled changes use layer visibility and do not reload the map style.
9. Michael approved mode-specific useful zoom limits after physical-device testing.
   - Aerial mode is capped at zoom 20 to prevent the severe pixel enlargement seen
     at zoom 22 while retaining useful pasture-level inspection.
   - Labeled mode is capped at its source limit of zoom 16.
   - Selecting Labeled above zoom 16 preserves the map center and eases back to 16.
   - A compact numeric zoom readout remains visible for device validation.
10. RW-TX-003 uses a single ephemeral water point and a 243.84-meter geodesic ring.
    - Placement is explicitly armed; the next map tap places or replaces the point.
    - Unarmed taps do not alter the point, and Clear removes both overlay features.
    - No database, preferences, file, or cloud persistence is introduced in this phase.
11. Michael approved the RW-TX-004 persistence and multi-water interaction direction.
    - Android Room with KSP is the approved native offline persistence engine.
    - Multiple water assets will be stored locally and exposed as Flow-backed state.
    - New assets receive automatic sequential display names such as "Water Point 1";
      the operator may rename them afterward rather than being interrupted at placement.
    - Tapping an existing marker opens a compact bottom card for inspection, editing,
      and confirmed deletion.
    - Overlapping 800-foot rings remain intentionally visible because their blended
      coverage communicates connected zones and possible grazing-pressure overlap.
    - Cloud accounts, synchronization, billing, live GPS, and pasture polygons remain
      outside RW-TX-004 unless Michael separately approves them.
12. Michael ratified the core RW-TX-005 pasture-boundary drawing workflow.
    - The operator taps Draw Pasture to enter an explicit drawing mode.
    - Successive map taps place fence-corner vertices connected by visible line segments.
    - Drawing controls provide Undo Last Point, Cancel, and Finish.
    - Finish remains unavailable until at least three vertices exist.
    - Finishing automatically closes the polygon and then prompts for a pasture name.
    - Completed pasture boundaries persist locally in Room.
    - Saved pastures can later be selected, renamed, reshaped, and deleted.
    - Boundary styling is ratified: bright magenta #FF2D95 inner line with near-black
      #151515 outer casing, matching fill near 8% opacity, and white-centered magenta
      vertex handles during drawing and reshaping. Selected pastures use a thicker line
      and expose their editable corner handles.
    - Acreage calculation and presentation are ratified. RangeWater closes the GeoJSON
      polygon, rejects fewer than three unique vertices and self-crossing geometry, and
      calculates approximate geodesic area with TurfMeasurement.area().
    - Turf square meters are converted using 4,046.8564224 square meters per acre.
      Acreage updates after every add, undo, or vertex move; the UI displays one decimal
      place while calculations retain full precision.
    - Boundary coordinates are the durable source of truth. Acreage is derived whenever
      the geometry loads or changes and is labeled "Map estimate — not a surveyed boundary."
    - Pasture-boundary editing is ratified. Selecting a saved pasture opens a bottom
      card with its name, acreage, Edit Boundary, Rename, and confirmed Delete actions.
    - Edit Boundary is an explicit mode with large existing-vertex handles, an explicit
      Add Corner then tap-the-fence workflow, drag-to-reposition, vertex selection and
      removal, and Undo, Cancel, and Save controls. A polygon may never fall below three
      vertices. This explicit insertion mode supersedes RW-TX-005 midpoint handles.
    - Cancel restores the pre-edit geometry. Save rejects self-crossing or otherwise
      invalid geometry and recalculates acreage before persistence.
    - Water overlays remain visible but non-interactive during pasture drawing or editing.
    - Ordinary-mode selection priority is ratified: a rendered water marker wins its hit
      test first; otherwise a tap inside a pasture selects that pasture. Drawing/editing
      modes suspend both ordinary selection paths.
    - Persistent schema is ratified: PastureEntity stores durable identity, generated
      default name, optional notes, and audit timestamps; ordered PastureVertexEntity
      rows store each coordinate with a parent foreign key and cascade deletion.
    - Pasture vertices also reserve nullable elevationMeters, elevationSource,
      verticalDatum, verticalAccuracyMeters, and elevationCapturedAt fields. RW-TX-005
      stores the fields but does not implement DEM lookup, GPS capture, grade, or terrain
      analysis.
    - Room advances from version 1 to version 2 through an explicit non-destructive
      Migration(1, 2). Android migration tests must prove existing water points survive.
    - Boundary coordinates are authoritative; acreage is derived. Unsaved drawing/editing
      drafts remain in memory until Save. Future gates use separate entities and reference
      pasture IDs rather than being embedded in pasture rows.
    - Existing water overlays remain unmodified and unclipped in RW-TX-005. Coverage
      percentages, tiered green/yellow water zones, gates, routes, terrain analysis, and
      animal profiles remain downstream work.
13. Michael approved explicit repositioning of an existing water asset in RW-TX-005-REV2.
    - Move Location enters an isolated interaction mode that suspends ordinary water and
      pasture selection.
    - Map taps update an in-memory preview of the selected pin and its derived 800-foot
      geodesic ring without writing tentative coordinates to Room.
    - Cancel discards the preview and restores the original Room-backed position without
      a database write.
    - Save updates only latitude, longitude, and updatedAt through the existing DAO while
      preserving the asset ID, name, type, notes, and createdAt.
    - No schema version, migration, dependency, or overlay-layer change is introduced.
14. Michael ratified tiered water coverage in RW-TX-006-WTR.
    - 0 through 800 feet (243.84 meters) is the translucent green preferred-access zone.
    - 800 through 1,000 feet (243.84 through 304.80 meters) is the translucent yellow
      planning-transition zone, constructed as a true 200-foot annulus with an inner hole.
    - Beyond 1,000 feet has no coverage fill. The 1,000-foot boundary is not described
      as a scientifically proven biological danger threshold.
    - Default water pins remain cyan; selected pins remain amber with a strong white halo.
    - Overlapping zones blend naturally, and Move Location previews both derived zones.
    - Coverage remains derived from saved coordinates; Room stays at version 2 with no
      new persistence fields, migration, or external dependency.
    - Z-order remains pasture fill, water coverage, pasture boundaries, edit handles,
      selected-water halo, then water pins.
15. Michael approved water-coverage visibility controls for RW-TX-006-WTR-REV1.
    - Full shows coverage fills and outlines; Lines Only hides fills but keeps outlines;
      Off hides coverage geometry while water pins remain visible and interactive.
    - Pasture Fill is independently adjustable. Its layer stays active at zero opacity
      when hidden so pasture tap selection continues to work; boundaries remain visible.
    - Display choices persist outside Room. Move Location temporarily forces Full coverage
      for placement feedback, then Cancel or Save restores the operator's chosen mode.

🔧 BLOCK 5 — UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Choose the offline regional-package format and provisioning path, including
   MapLibre OfflineManager, MBTiles, PMTiles, or another validated format.
2. Record live-service operational limits separately from the public-domain status
   of the underlying CONUS imagery data.
3. Choose the elevation and slope pipeline: local DEM tiles or cached USGS 3DEP data.
   - Proposed future grade analysis records nullable elevation, source, vertical datum,
     reported accuracy, and capture timestamp with each pasture vertex.
   - Segment grade requires both elevation change and geodesic horizontal distance:
     grade percent = elevation change / horizontal distance x 100.
   - USGS 3DEP lookup, device GNSS, and manual/survey elevations must remain identified
     by source and datum; values from unlike vertical references must not be mixed.
   - Fence-post grades are segment estimates. Terrain-aware livestock-path and >15%
     slope analysis requires denser DEM sampling between vertices, not endpoints alone.
4. Verify the exact evidence trail behind the 800-foot behavioral interpretation.
5. Measure USDA dynamic-service latency on target devices and decide whether a
   RangeWater-controlled proxy cache or regional offline package is needed.
6. Product option under consideration: keep federal imagery as the capable free
   baseline and evaluate a future paid tier using properly licensed higher-resolution
   commercial imagery. Provider, licensing, entitlement, pricing, and billing remain
   undecided; no paid-imagery commitment is made by RW-TX-003.
7. Proposed downstream pasture-connectivity model (not yet ratified):
   - Adjacent or divided pastures remain separate polygons, including when they share
     a fence line.
   - A gate may become a durable boundary-linked asset connecting two pastures, with a
     proposed opening width of 8 through 32 feet. Presets, custom widths, gate types,
     status, snapping rules, and schema remain undecided.
8. Proposed livestock-movement routes (not yet ratified):
   - Operators may trace a directional path through one or more gates to represent a
     planned cattle movement between pastures.
   - Route persistence, arrows, naming, validation, and editing remain undecided and
     depend on approved pasture and gate models.
9. Proposed animal profiles and optional distance rules (not yet ratified):
   - Future profiles may support cattle, horses, goats, sheep, bison, and other stock.
   - Each profile requires evidence-backed distance thresholds and must not inherit the
     cattle 800-foot baseline without validation.
10. Proposed pasture-aware accessible-coverage model (recorded, not yet ratified):
    - Physical Radius continues to show complete geodesic distance geometry for planning.
    - Accessible Coverage clips derived water zones to explicitly assigned pasture polygons.
    - A water asset may serve zero, one, or multiple pastures. A split waterer may serve
      adjoining fields; a shared pond or trough may serve several assigned fields.
    - Unassigned water remains in Physical Radius view so the app does not invent access.
    - Permanent versus portable describes water-asset mobility and remains separate from
      the Serves Pastures relationship that describes livestock access.
    - Future pasture percentages must intersect assigned water zones with the pasture and
      union overlaps before measuring area, preventing the same acre from being counted
      more than once. Schema, clipping engine, assignment UI, and migration remain open.

RangeWater is an offline-capable Android utility for cattle producers and rotational
grazers. It maps preferred-access and planning-transition distances around water assets
to help producers plan trough placement, fence layouts, and pasture utilization.
